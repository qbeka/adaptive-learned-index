Details

This is a C++20 ordered index for 64-bit keys. A small line predicts where a key sits, a bounded search corrects the guess, and new keys wait in a buffer beside each leaf, while a cost-based policy decides when merging that buffer is worth the work.

My preregistered claim failed. The adaptive policy did not beat a fixed rebuild threshold on the primary data. At full scale, rebuilds barely paid and ALEX stayed 1.2x to 3.4x faster.

The replay

Every key, prediction and cost above comes from a recorded trace: wiki_ts_200M_uint64 at stride 2048, the shifting_hotspot workload, seed 102, 20,000,000 operations, digest 63e7fc3fbf8b9bf2. The comparisons are replayed from those keys with the index's own search.

This is the first rebuild in that trace small enough to draw, so it is unlikely to be a typical one. Costs were frozen so the recording repeats, which makes 1,836 ns and 1,480 ns the policy's estimates rather than timings.

Limits

  • Single-threaded and in-memory, with no concurrency, durability or production use.
  • It does not beat ALEX or PGM on throughput or lookup time on full data.
  • The policy did not beat fixed thresholds overall.
  • The 1.300 is exploratory, and preregistered applies to the update experiment only.
  • Most of the bytes that rebuilds saved were directory shifting rather than key copying.
